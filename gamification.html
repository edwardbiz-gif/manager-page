📐 The design principles all 5 concepts follow (from the research)

Design Principles — the spine every concept must share

These are the non-negotiable design choices distilled from the motivation, gamification, and incentive-design research, tuned to a 3-veteran team, monthly cash, uneven 5/4/3 patches. All five concepts must satisfy these regardless of their surface mechanic.

P1 — Frame money as competence-feedback, not a do-X-for-$Y quota. These are experienced operators with professional pride. Performance-contingent cash on already-valued work crowds out intrinsic drive (Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999 meta of 128 studies; overjustification). Every concept must lead with recognition, scorecard movement, and owner acknowledgment, and leave supervisors autonomy over which gap to attack first. The dollars are the proof of competence, not the headline.

P2 — Pay enough or do not bother; never fragment the pool. Tiny contingent rewards perform *worse* than no reward (Gneezy/Rustichini 2000, V-shaped curve). With a ~$1,500–$3,000/month total pool, each supervisor's realistically-achievable stake must land at ~$500–$1,000/month. Do not splinter into $50-per-KPI crumbs. Concentrate.

P3 — Score on IMPROVEMENT and GAP-CLOSING, never raw levels. This is both the fairness lever (P-Fairness) and the motivation lever. Confirmed against the real data: raw patch OSAT is nearly tied (Randy 63.5 / Manny 64.2 / Nick 65.7), so a raw-level race is a coin flip that rewards whoever inherited the best stores. Score each *store* on (a) month-over-month delta and (b) distance-to-target, normalize, then roll to patch. This gives Boulder Hill (OSAT 58.8) and Yorkville (69.4) both a live path to win.

P4 — Balanced multi-KPI basket; no single metric dominates. Goodhart's Law + the multitask principal-agent problem (Holmstrom/Milgrom 1991): a single high-powered target starves everything unmeasured. Every concept uses 5–6 connected KPIs — OSAT gap, cleanliness, complaint-recovery/SLA, labor-in-band, kitchen, and one GPS activity signal — with no KPI worth more than ~30% of the pool. Weight OSAT and complaint-recovery highest because those are the org's worst gaps (OSAT 63.8 vs 70.1 BU benchmark; 89% of 54 complaints past 72h SLA).

P5 — Mostly ABSOLUTE pool, only a small relative sweetener. A pure 1-2-3 rank tournament among three colleagues who must cooperate daily invites sabotage, knowledge-hoarding, and collusion (Lazear/Rosen; CEPR on relative-performance sabotage). Most dollars must come from each supervisor hitting their own targets (all three can win the same month). Keep at most a small "most-improved" topper (multi-prize, never winner-take-all) plus an explicit reward for sharing a winning tactic across patches.

P6 — Compete against the benchmark, cooperate within the team. Frame the contest as "close the org's OSAT 63.8→70.1 gap" and "clear the 89% SLA breach" — an external common enemy, not supervisor-vs-supervisor. Intergroup-competition + within-group-cooperation beats pure competition on both enjoyment and performance. A shared/team component lets all three win together when org gaps close.

P7 — Gain framing only; no prepay-and-clawback. Loss-framed incentives backfire in the field — Rees-Jones et al. (NBER w26619, 294 car dealers) found loss framing *cut* sales ~5%. Core payout is a gain-framed earned bonus. Any loss element (a breakable streak) must be small and symbolic, and a broken streak must never forfeit a whole month's pay.

P8 — Live progress UI with goal-gradient + endowed progress. A month has a natural finish line. Render a per-supervisor progress bar on the GPS/manager-page UI with some progress pre-credited and a "distance to next tier" cue (Kivetz/Urminsky/Zheng 2006). This drives the end-of-month push without changing payout economics.

P9 — Make the game the work, not a scoreboard bolted on (beat novelty decay). PBL skins spike then fade within weeks. Tie scoring to the actual workflow — GPS visits, complaint-recovery touches, photo submissions — so the loop self-reinforces (Fogg B=MAP: easy + prompted; Hook loop). Rotate the monthly focus metric so the game stays alive across a recurring monthly cadence.

P10 — Always state the data-through date; flag every lag. House rule and trust requirement. No bare numbers. Lock each month's scorecard on a fixed cutoff (~day 3–5 of the next month, after R365 labor's ~1.5-day lag settles) and stamp the data-through date on every payout.

P11 — Confirm FLSA-exempt status before launch. A pre-announced formula bonus is non-discretionary by definition. If supervisors are exempt (likely for area supervisors) it does not trigger regular-rate/OT recalculation; if any participant is non-exempt — or when this extends to shift-leaders/crew — the bonus folds into the regular rate and OT must be recalculated per workweek. Verify now; flag hard before any crew expansion.

F1 — Normalize to patch level, so patch SIZE is neutral. Score each *store* first (per-store improvement + gap-to-target), then average to patch (not sum). Averaging means Randy's 5 stores and Nick's 3 stores compete on equal footing; nobody is advantaged or penalized for headcount. Open-complaint *totals* (24/17/13) must be rated as per-store rates or recovery rates, never raw counts.

F2 — Improvement + gap-to-target is the scoring axis, not raw level. Because raw averages are tied (F-table), only how far each patch *moves toward benchmark* distinguishes real performance. This unlocks the weak patches: Boulder Hill (OSAT 58.8, cleanliness 42.2) has the most room and so the most earnable improvement points, while Yorkville (69.4) and Farnsworth (81.1) can still earn by holding/extending their lead via the "sustained level" credit.

F3 — Give weaker starting positions an accounting head-start. Where two patches put in equal effort, equal reward must follow regardless of starting level. Use a handicap on the gap-closing component (distance-from-target scaled) so a supervisor inheriting low stores isn't structurally locked out and one inheriting strong stores isn't locked in. Nick starts closest to the 72 line (6.3 gap) — the head-start ensures Randy (8.5 gap) is competing on slope, not altitude.

F4 — Everyone can earn the full target every month (absolute-first). Per P5: most of the pool is absolute ("beat your own threshold"), so all three veterans can max their own payout in the same month without taking from each other. The only zero-sum element is a small most-improved topper. No supervisor publicly "loses" each month — critical for an N=3 peer group who must cooperate daily.

F5 — No public 1-2-3 leaderboard. Surface each supervisor's personal-best and gap-to-next-tier privately, plus the shared "team vs benchmark" view. An absolute rank board makes 2 of 3 veterans lose every month and corrodes peer trust (the documented N=3 failure mode).

F6 — Reward both improvement AND held gains. A patch that's already strong (Farnsworth, Yorkville) must have an honest path via a "sustained-excellence" credit, so fairness cuts both ways — the weak aren't locked out *and* the strong aren't punished for having less room to improve.

F7 — Rotate the monthly focus so no patch's weak spot is permanently the deciding metric. Rotating which gap leads each month (cleanliness one month, complaint-recovery the next) keeps the contest from systematically favoring whichever patch happens to be strong on a fixed headline KPI.

Anti-Gaming Guardrails — Goodhart defense every concept must implement

Gaming is not hypothetical for these exact KPIs. Each guardrail below is mandatory; concepts may add more but may not drop any.

G1 — Pair every incentivized KPI with an opposing counter-metric. If a target can improve while real harm rises, the metric set is incomplete. Required pairings:

  • Labor% ⇄ OSAT + kitchen. Labor-in-band credit only counts if OSAT and kitchen *hold or improve*. A supervisor must not be able to win by cutting hours that tank service. (Plano at 29.05% labor and Oswego 2 at 27.99% are the obvious squeeze targets — guard them.)
  • Complaint-CLOSE ⇄ intake/reopen audit. Closing complaints counts only against an audited open-rate and a reopened-within-30-days check. This is the single biggest suppression risk given today's 89% SLA breach.
  • Sales/GC ⇄ cleanliness + OSAT. Sales pushes can't come at the cost of the experience.

G2 — Require genuine complaint-RECOVERY, not just closure. Score the *recovery touch* (a logged contact with the guest), not the act of marking a ticket closed. No closing-without-recovery earns credit. This directly attacks complaint suppression, the highest-risk exploit.

G3 — OSAT survey-volume floor. A patch must not win by shrinking the denominator (steering away dissatisfied guests, discouraging surveys). Set a minimum survey count per store per month; below it, OSAT credit is voided for that store.

G4 — Freeze baselines on a trailing window; publish a quarter ahead. Never auto-reset to last month. The ratchet/success-penalty (Weitzman) gives supervisors a perverse incentive to sandbag so next month's bar is lower ("fulfil the plan 105%, never 125%"). Use a fixed trailing-multi-month baseline, pre-published for the full quarter, and reward sustained level, not one-month pops.

G5 — GPS activity must be quality-validated, not presence-only. Geofenced check-ins can be gamed with drive-bys to hold a streak. Pair every check-in with dwell time + follow-through evidence (a flagged task touched, a photo submitted, a recovery logged). Reward the touch, never the ping alone.

G6 — Cap any single metric at ~30% of the pool. Low-powered weights across a connected basket mean you can't move one number without honestly moving the underlying reality (the documented Goodhart defense). No headline metric.

G7 — Run a pre-mortem before launch. For each KPI, write down how a clever supervisor could move the number without improving reality, and confirm a guardrail blocks it. Instrument everything (all data is in Supabase) so Eddie can measure whether the game actually moves OSAT/cleanliness/complaints rather than assuming.

G8 — Integrity gate, not just point deductions. Detected suppression, denominator-shrinking, or fake check-ins void the affected metric's credit for the month (a gate), rather than a small subtractive penalty — because a small penalty still leaves gaming +EV. Gating makes the exploit worthless.

🧠 Motivation & evidence

The lever: competence + status + social comparison, defanged of its downside

The Patch League leans hardest on the three motivators that move experienced professionals: competence (Self-Determination Theory — these are veterans who want proof they are good at the job), status (Festinger's social-comparison theory — people calibrate their own worth against similar others, and three peer supervisors are the textbook "similar others"), and the drive of a real contest (it is more engaging than a solo checklist).

The research is blunt about why a naive leaderboard backfires here. With only three players, a public 1-2-3 absolute rank means two of three veterans "lose" every single month — corrosive to a peer group who must cooperate daily, and a documented driver of sabotage and knowledge-hoarding (Lazear/Rosen tournament theory; CEPR on relative-performance sabotage). So this concept keeps the league *feel* while engineering out the poison:

  • It is a HANDICAPPED league (think golf handicaps or a pro sports salary-floor): the score is normalized for patch size and starting altitude, so the contest is on *slope, not altitude*. Confirmed on the real data — raw patch OSAT is a near-tie (Randy 63.5 / Manny 64.2 / Nick 65.7), so an un-handicapped race is a coin flip that rewards inherited stores. The handicap means Boulder Hill (58.8) and Yorkville (69.4) both have a live path to win (Principle P3, Fairness F2/F3).
  • Most of the money is absolute, the league is the topper (P5/P2): 75% of the pool comes from each supervisor beating their *own* pre-published targets — all three can max out together. The competitive "Patch Cup" is a 25% sweetener, and even the third-place finisher gets paid (no zero-payout shame, mitigating the N=3 trap — F4/F5).
  • The common enemy is the benchmark, not each other (P6): the league standings sit beside a shared "Team vs Business-Unit" bar (org OSAT 63.8 → 70.1). When the team closes the org gap, a team bonus unlocks for everyone — competition for status, cooperation for cash.

Evidence basis: Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999 (competence framing, overjustification) → money as proof-of-competence not quota. Gneezy/Rustichini 2000 (V-curve) → concentrate the stake at $500–$1,000. Festinger 1954 (social comparison) → the league engine. Lazear/Rosen + CEPR → the anti-sabotage redesign. Kivetz/Urminsky/Zheng 2006 (goal-gradient/endowed progress) → the live standings table with pre-credited progress.

⚙️ How it works (mechanics)

How The Patch League is scored

The unit of scoring is the store, then averaged to the patch (Fairness F1 — neutralizes 5/4/3 size). Open-complaint counts are converted to per-store recovery *rates*, never raw totals.

Step 1 — Per-store KPI points (the "LeagueScore")

Six KPIs. Each store earns points on a blend of (a) month-over-month improvement and (b) distance-to-target, with a handicap weight so a point of improvement is worth more the further the store starts below target:

store_kpi_points = improvement × (1 + gap_to_target/10) + sustained_level_credit

  • gap_to_target uses the frozen trailing-3-month baseline (G4), not last month — kills sandbagging.
  • sustained_level_credit rewards stores already at/above target for holding the lead (F6) — so Farnsworth (OSAT 81) and Yorkville (69) are not locked out by having little room to improve.
  • Worked OSAT example confirmed on real data: River Road (+2.1 from 55.0, weight 2.70) earns +5.67 pts; Farnsworth (+0.5 from 80.6) earns +5.87 via sustained-level credit — a far-from-target mover and an already-elite holder both score top of the league.
Step 2 — KPI weights (no metric > 30% — Guardrail G6)

OSAT 25% · Complaint-recovery 25% · Cleanliness 15% · Kitchen 15% · Labor-in-band 10% (gated) · GPS-visit-quality 10%. OSAT and recovery weighted highest because they are the org's worst gaps. The monthly focus KPI rotates (P9/F7): the leading KPI gets a +5% weight bump that month (e.g. "Cleanliness Month"), so no patch's fixed weak spot is permanently decisive.

Step 3 — Two payout engines
  1. League Standard (75% of pool, ABSOLUTE): each KPI has a pre-published per-supervisor target. Supervisor earns their share of the absolute pool proportional to targets hit. All three can earn the full amount the same month.
  2. Patch Cup (25%, RELATIVE topper): patches ranked 1-2-3 by total handicapped LeagueScore. Multi-prize 50/33/17 split — even last place is paid (F5).
  3. Team-vs-Benchmark unlock (recognition + small cash): if org OSAT closes ≥1.0 pt toward 70.1 that month, a flat +$75 per supervisor unlocks (P6 cooperation). A cross-patch tactic-sharing bounty ($100) goes to any supervisor whose logged tactic another adopts and that lifts the other's KPI.
Cadence & lock

Scorecard locks on day 4 of the following month (after R365 labor's ~1.5-day lag settles — P10). Every standings view stamps the data-through date. Pays monthly via Paycor, separate from the quarterly bonus engine.

🛡️ Anti-gaming safeguards

Anti-gaming (all shared guardrails implemented, plus league-specific)

  • G1 paired counter-metrics: Labor-in-band credit is gated on OSAT + kitchen holding/improving (Plano 29.05% and Oswego 2 27.99% are the watched squeeze targets — a supervisor cannot win by cutting hours that tank service). Sales/GC credit gated on cleanliness+OSAT. Complaint-close gated on intake/reopen audit.
  • G2 genuine recovery: points come from a logged guest-recovery touch, not marking a ticket closed. Closing-without-recovery earns zero. Directly attacks the 89% SLA-breach suppression risk.
  • G3 OSAT survey-volume floor: a store below its minimum monthly survey count has OSAT credit voided — can't win by shrinking the denominator.
  • G4 frozen trailing baseline, published a quarter ahead: never auto-reset to last month (kills the ratchet/sandbag). Sustained level is rewarded, not one-month pops.
  • G5 GPS quality-validated: a check-in counts only with dwell + follow-through evidence (a flagged task touched, photo submitted, recovery logged) — no drive-by streak farming.
  • G6 cap 30%: no KPI exceeds 25% base (30% in its rotation month). You cannot move the league without honestly moving multiple realities.
  • G7 pre-mortem: documented per-KPI exploit list before launch; instrument all six in Supabase so Eddie measures whether real OSAT/cleanliness/complaints move.
  • G8 integrity GATE, not deduction: detected suppression/fake-check-in/denominator-shrinking voids that metric's credit for the month — makes the exploit worthless rather than merely penalized.
  • League-specific anti-sabotage: because the Cup is relative, the tactic-sharing bounty and team-unlock actively *reward* helping a rival, counteracting the leaderboard's sabotage incentive (CEPR finding). No public standings (see UX) further reduces the incentive to undermine a peer.

🧠 Motivation & evidence

Why "Beat Your Best" is the right lever for this team

The core insight: raw levels are a coin flip, so race the self. Verified on the real Jun-15 data, patch OSAT is essentially tied — Randy 63.5 / Manny 64.2 / Nick 65.7. A raw-level contest would just pay whoever inherited the best stores. "Beat Your Best" scores every store against *its own* rolling 3-month baseline, so the question is never "who has the nicest patch" but "who moved their patch the most this month." That makes effort, not inheritance, the thing that pays (Principle P3, Fairness F2).

Lever 1 — Mastery / Competence (Self-Determination Theory; Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999). These are experienced operators with professional pride. The most durable motivator for experts is *visible mastery progress*, not a cash quota. "Beat your own record" is the purest mastery frame there is — it speaks the language of a craftsman improving, and the money reads as proof of competence, not a bribe, which avoids the overjustification/crowding-out trap that performance-contingent cash creates on already-valued work (P1).

Lever 2 — Goal-gradient + endowed progress (Hull 1932; Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006). Effort accelerates as a visible finish line nears. A calendar month is a natural finish line; a personal baseline is a personal start line. We render a per-supervisor progress bar with the baseline pre-credited as "endowed progress," so each supervisor starts the month already partway up their own bar and pushes hardest in the final week (P8). Because the bar is *personal*, nobody is ever demoralized by being behind a peer.

Lever 3 — Loss aversion, used carefully (Kahneman & Tversky 1979). Beating your best builds a streak; losing your best breaks it. Losses loom ~2x larger than gains, which is exactly why a personal-best streak is so sticky. But the field evidence is a hard warning: loss-FRAMED *pay* backfires (Rees-Jones et al., NBER w26619, 294 dealers, ~5% sales drop). So the loss element is streak-and-status only, never clawed-back cash (P7): the dollars are always gain-framed earned bonus; only the symbolic streak badge can break, and even that has grace months.

Lever 4 — Compete against the benchmark, cooperate within the team (intergroup-competition research; Lazear/Rosen tournament cautions). Because the scored opponent is *past-you*, three colleagues who must cooperate daily are never set head-to-head for the bulk of the money (P5, P6). All three can hit excellence in the same month. A small "Most-Improved" topper adds a sprinkle of friendly rivalry without the sabotage/collusion failure mode of a winner-take-all tournament among N=3 peers.

Why this beats the alternatives for THIS team: with only three veterans of near-tied raw performance and uneven 5/4/3 patches, any level-based or rank-based design either rewards inheritance or guarantees two losers a month. Self-referential improvement is the one frame that is simultaneously fair (everyone races an equal-difficulty opponent), motivating (mastery + goal-gradient), and cooperation-safe (no zero-sum core).

⚙️ How it works (mechanics)

How "Beat Your Best" scores — concrete enough to implement

The unit of scoring is the STORE, rolled up to the PATCH by AVERAGE

Each store is scored 0–100 on each KPI vs its own rolling baseline, the KPI scores are weighted into a store score, then store scores are averaged (not summed) to a patch score. Averaging makes patch size neutral — Randy's 5 and Nick's 3 compete on equal footing (Fairness F1).

The baseline (this is the heart of the anti-sandbag design)
  • Baseline = trailing 3-month average, frozen and pre-published a full quarter ahead (Guardrail G4). You cannot lower next month's bar by tanking this month, because next month's bar was published before this month started.
  • Baselines update each quarter as a rolling window, but the score also pays a sustained-level credit (below), so holding a high level is rewarded — you don't lose the bonus just because your baseline caught up to your performance (Fairness F6). This closes the ratchet/Weitzman sandbag loop: there is no upside to "fulfil 105%, never 125%."
Per-KPI score (0–100) — the Beat-Your-Best curve

For each KPI a store earns:

  • Decline (below baseline): 40 + (delta × penalty), floored at 0. Each point down hurts.
  • Hold (= baseline): 40 — "held the line" credit.
  • Improve (above baseline, below target): ramps 40 → 62 for a *solid* monthly move, then 62 → ~98 for a *great* move. "Solid" and "great" are defined per KPI in points (e.g. OSAT solid = +1.5, great = +4.0; recovery solid = +5pts, great = +15pts).
  • Sustained excellence (baseline already at/above target, e.g. Farnsworth OSAT 81): holding earns 70, extending earns up to 100 — so strong stores aren't locked out for having no room (Fairness F6).
  • Head-start handicap: improvement credit is multiplied by up to 1.5× for low-baseline stores (scaled by distance-to-target, capped). A point gained at Boulder Hill (OSAT 55) is worth more than a point at Yorkville (69.4) because it was harder-won room (Fairness F3).

🛡️ Anti-gaming safeguards

Anti-gaming — every guardrail from the shared spec, plus the baseline defense this concept owns

The signature defense — sandbagging is structurally dead (G4). Baselines are a frozen trailing-3-month window pre-published a full quarter ahead. You physically cannot lower next month's bar by tanking this month — the bar was set before the month began. And the sustained-excellence credit removes the ratchet incentive entirely: holding a high level keeps paying, so there is no reason to "never beat your best by too much." This is the exploit the concept's name most invites, and it is closed by design.

G1 — Counter-metric pairing (opposing guardrails):

  • *Labor% ⇄ OSAT + kitchen:* labor-in-band credit (10%) counts ONLY if OSAT and kitchen hold or improve vs baseline. In the worked example this voided labor credit at Plano (29.05%, OSAT slipping) and Oswego 2 (27.99%) — exactly the two squeeze targets flagged.
  • *Complaint-close ⇄ intake/reopen audit:* closing counts only against audited open-rate and a reopened-within-30-days check.
  • *Sales/GC ⇄ cleanliness + OSAT:* sales movement never scores if cleanliness/OSAT decline.

G2 — Reward RECOVERY, not closure. The 25% complaint weight scores the logged guest-recovery touch, not ticket-marking. Closing without a recorded recovery earns nothing. Directly attacks the highest-risk exploit (suppression) given today's 89% SLA breach.

G3 — OSAT survey-volume floor. Below a per-store minimum survey count, that store's OSAT credit is voided — you can't win by shrinking the denominator / steering away unhappy guests.

G5 — GPS validated, not presence-only. Every geofenced check-in must pair with dwell time + follow-through evidence (a flagged task touched, a photo submitted, a recovery logged). Drive-by pings earn nothing.

G6 — No metric > 30% of pool. OSAT caps at 30%; the balanced basket means you can't move the score without honestly moving underlying reality.

G7 — Pre-mortem + full instrumentation. Before launch, for each KPI write the cheat and confirm a guardrail blocks it. All signals live in Supabase, so Eddie measures whether the game actually moved OSAT/cleanliness/complaints, not assumes it.

G8 — Integrity GATE, not point-shaving. Detected suppression, denominator-shrinking, or fake check-ins void the affected metric's credit for the month (a gate), so the exploit's EV is zero — a small subtractive penalty would still leave gaming +EV.

A specific, hard, time-boxed goal ("close OSAT toward 72 this month") produces more effort than "improve everything." The month is a natural finish line (P8 goal-gradient).

Variety + variable reward beats novelty decay (Hook model; operant variable-ratio schedules). The #1 failure of gamification is the skin fading in weeks (P9). How Mission Control scores — concrete enough to implement

The three buckets (of a $2,400/mo pool)

  2. Always-On Floor — 20% ($480, $160/sup). The standing 5-check guardrail basket (keeps everything else honest; this is the balanced-scorecard part, P4/G1).

Mission Bounty scoring (the heart)

For the spotlight KPI, score each store then average to patch (F1 — size-neutral):

StoreMissionScore = 0.6 × ImprovementNorm + 0.4 × GapCloseNorm

🛡️ Anti-gaming safeguards

Anti-gaming — Goodhart defense for Mission Control

The rotating-spotlight design has one structural risk a flat scorecard doesn't: the non-spotlight KPIs could be neglected during their off-months. The Always-On Floor (20%, 5 standing checks) exists precisely to stop that — every guardrail KPI is *always* scored even when it isn't the mission, so you can't let cleanliness rot while chasing OSAT. All shared guardrails (G1-G8) are implemented:

  • G1 paired counter-metrics. Labor-in-band credit (Always-On check 3) only counts if OSAT and kitchen hold/improve — directly guards the squeeze targets Plano (29.05%) and Oswego 2 (27.99%). Sales pushes are checked against cleanliness+OSAT. Complaint-close is checked against an intake/reopen audit.
  • G2 recovery, not closure. When the mission *is* complaint-SLA, and always in the Always-On basket, credit is earned on a logged recovery touch with the guest, never on marking a ticket closed. Closing-without-recovery earns zero. This is the #1 exploit given today's 89% breach rate.
  • G3 OSAT survey-volume floor. Each store must clear a minimum monthly survey count; below it, that store's OSAT mission credit is voided — can't win by shrinking the denominator.
  • G4 frozen, pre-published baselines. Baselines are a trailing 3-month window, and the full quarterly mission rotation is published in advance so nobody sandbags one month to lower next month's bar. Mission scores the move toward an *absolute target* (72), not vs last month's pop, so a sustained level is what pays.
  • G5 GPS quality-validated. GPS check-ins (Always-On check 5) require dwell time + a follow-through artifact (flagged task touched, photo submitted, recovery logged). A drive-by ping alone earns nothing.
  • G6 30% cap respected. Mission Bounty is 70% but it rotates across KPIs, so no single *metric* is permanently above 30% of annual pay — over a quarter, OSAT ≈ 35-40% of bounty months, others share the rest, and the standing basket spreads the floor. (If Eddie wants a strict monthly ≤30% cap, split the 70% into 40% mission + 30% a second co-spotlight KPI.)
  • G7 pre-mortem per mission. Each month's mission ships with a written "how could this be gamed" note and the matching guardrail confirmed live before launch. All signals instrumented in Supabase so Eddie measures whether OSAT/complaints actually moved.
  • G8 integrity gate. Detected suppression, denominator-shrinking, or fake check-ins void the affected metric's credit for the month (gate, not a small deduction) — making the exploit worthless, not merely +EV-reduced.

Mission-specific pre-mortem example (OSAT month): Risk = steering angry guests away from surveys to protect the score → blocked by G3 survey floor + G8 gate. Risk = cutting labor to "improve" perceived speed → blocked by G1 labor⇄OSAT/kitchen pairing.

🧠 Motivation & evidence

Why a cooperative cascade is the right lever for THIS org

The diagnosis says the problem is relational, not individual. The two worst org gaps — OSAT (63.8 vs 70.1 BU) and the 89% complaint-SLA breach — are not things a supervisor fixes from a car. They are fixed by GMs and crews on the floor, shift after shift. And the single most destructive number in the deck is ~93% annualized crew turnover: you cannot hold OSAT or cleanliness with a revolving door. Turnover is a *team-cohesion* failure. The right motivational lever is therefore relatedness, not individual incentive.

Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan). Relatedness is one of the three basic psychological needs alongside autonomy and competence. A cooperative structure where the supervisor's reward is bound to their GMs' and crews' success directly feeds relatedness — the lever the other four concepts touch least. This is the concept that builds the *team*, not just the scorekeeper.

Shared-fate / common-goal cooperation (Deutsch's theory of cooperation & competition; Sherif Robbers Cave). When rewards are positively interdependent, people help each other, share information, and coordinate. "All Hands" makes the supervisor and GM *positively interdependent by design*: the GM's store improving is literally money in the supervisor's pocket and vice-versa. This is the documented antidote to the knowledge-hoarding and sabotage that pure rank tournaments produce among an N=3 peer group (Lazear/Rosen; CEPR on relative-performance sabotage) — see principle P5/P6.

Goal-setting + line-of-sight (Locke & Latham; the "line-of-sight" gainsharing literature, e.g. Scanlon/Improshare evidence). Gainsharing works best when employees can see how their daily actions move the shared pot. Cascading the payout to the people who did the work closes that loop: the crew sees a clean lobby → store OSAT rises → patch pot grows → they get paid. This is also why we fold in the existing crew Kitchen Quality Race rather than competing with it — the Race becomes one funded rung of the same ladder.

Collective-incentive free-rider caveat, handled. The known weakness of team pay is free-riding in large groups. Here the groups are tiny (3-5 stores; a single store's crew), so social monitoring is high and the free-rider problem is small — exactly the regime where team incentives outperform. We still gate the cascade on each store's own contribution so no store rides on a sibling.

Net thesis: money framed as competence-recognition (P1) that flows through relatedness. The dollars prove the *team* is winning, the supervisor is the coach who is rewarded for building a winning team, and the org's worst gaps — the ones that are structurally team problems (turnover → OSAT → complaints) — are attacked at the level where they actually live.

🧠 Motivation & evidence

Why a points economy fits these three veterans

The lever: ownership + accumulation + endowed progress (Octalysis Core Drives 4 & 6, Goal-Gradient). Season Pass leans on the psychology of a *progress bar you own*. Points accumulate into a visible balance and a tier you've "earned," which triggers loss-aversion-adjacent endowment (you don't want to slip a tier) without ever using loss-framed pay (P7). Kivetz/Urminsky/Zheng (2006) endowed-progress: people accelerate toward a goal when given a head start — every supervisor starts each month with pre-credited points, so the bar is never at zero.

Variable/intermittent reward, bounded (Octalysis CD7 Unpredictability; Hook model). A small slice of the monthly upside is a *surprise variable reward* (a randomized "bonus point" multiplier on one rotating lane, revealed mid-month) — this is the dopamine engine that beats novelty decay (P9) without unbalancing budget, because the variable element only redistributes within a capped pool.

Competence-feedback framing, not a quota (P1; Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999). Points are scored on *already-valued professional work* — closing the OSAT gap, recovering guests, keeping stores clean. The score is the proof of mastery; the cash is downstream. Supervisors keep autonomy: they choose which lane to push to earn points, so it never reads as "do X for $Y."

Pay enough, concentrate (P2; Gneezy/Rustichini 2000 V-curve). Points convert to a ~$500-$950 monthly stake — never fragmented into per-KPI crumbs. The points *feel* granular (engagement), the money stays concentrated (motivation).

Improvement + gap-close, normalized to patch (P3, F1-F3). Confirmed on the real data: raw patch OSAT is a near-tie (Randy 63.5 / Manny 64.2 / Nick 65.7), so points are earned on movement and distance-to-target, with a head-start handicap for weaker stores. Compete vs the benchmark, cooperate within the team (P5, P6). Most points are absolute (all three can max out together); only a small "most-improved" topper and a tactic-sharing bonus are relative. The framed enemy is the org's 63.8→70.1 OSAT gap and the 89% SLA breach, not each other.

🛡️ Anti-gaming safeguards

Anti-gaming (every shared guardrail implemented)

  • G1 counter-metrics built into lanes. Labor-in-band points are gated: zero unless OSAT + kitchen held/improved (kills service-killing cuts; watch Plano/Oswego 2). Sales isn't a direct point lane (avoids sales-at-cost-of-experience); it lives in the existing comp plan.
  • G2 recovery, not closure. The complaint lane scores logged recovery touches per qualifying complaint, never ticket-marking. No touch = no points.
  • G3 OSAT survey floor. A store below its minimum monthly survey count (set from trailing volume) voids its OSAT points — can't win by shrinking the denominator.
  • G4 frozen, pre-published baselines. Baselines = fixed trailing-window, published a full quarter ahead; never auto-reset to last month (no sandbagging ratchet). The rotating focus lane is also pre-announced a quarter out.
  • G5 GPS quality-validated. Visit points require dwell time + follow-through evidence (flagged task / photo / recovery). Drive-by pings earn nothing.
  • G6 ~30% cap per lane. Top lane (OSAT) is 24% of the 125-pt max; no single metric dominates.
  • G7 pre-mortem. Before launch, write the exploit for each lane and confirm a guardrail blocks it; instrument all lanes in Supabase so Eddie can measure whether OSAT/cleanliness/complaints actually moved.
  • G8 integrity gate, not a nick. Detected suppression, denominator-shrinking, or fake check-ins void that lane's points for the month — gaming becomes worthless, not merely penalized.

Points-economy-specific risk: point inflation/grinding. Mitigated by the hard pool cap + pro-rata clamp (more points never means more total dollars than $3,000) and by lane caps (you can't grind one lane past its ceiling).

✅ Recommendation

Recommendation: Beat Your Best as the spine, with a small Mission Control spotlight folded in (the hybrid).

Why Beat Your Best wins outright. The data settles it: raw patch OSAT is a statistical tie (Randy 63.5 / Manny 64.2 / Nick 65.7) across uneven 5/4/3 patches. Any design scored on *levels* pays whoever inherited the best stores; any *rank* design with N=3 makes two proud veterans "lose" every month and breeds the sabotage/hoarding that tournament theory warns about in a peer group that must cooperate daily. Self-referential improvement is the only frame that is simultaneously fair (everyone races an equal-difficulty opponent — their own rolling 3-month baseline), gaming-resistant (a *rolling* baseline auto-corrects sandbagging — tank this month and you just lower the bar you must beat next month, and the broad 6-KPI basket plus the OSAT/kitchen gate on labor block cherry-picking and service-killing cuts), and cooperation-safe (the core dollars are absolute, so all three can max the same month; only a $300 most-improved topper is relative). It is also the lowest build lift — rolling baselines and a per-supervisor progress bar over data already in Supabase — which matters for a solo nights-and-weekends build with a July-1-adjacent bonus engine already on the critical path.

Why not the others as the primary. *Patch League* is the same fair/improvement engine but wraps it in a leaderboard whose 25% relative cup reintroduces exactly the N=3 friction Beat Your Best avoids — more risk for no extra fairness. *Mission Control* is the most exciting but its 70%-on-one-KPI core is the weakest on Goodhart: it structurally invites neglect of the five unspotlighted metrics within any given month — great as a seasoning, dangerous as the whole meal. *Season Pass* is the most engagement-durable but the heaviest build (points economy, tiers, variable-multiplier engine) for a marginal motivation gain over a clean progress bar — defer it. *All Hands* is the most strategically ambitious and the only concept that attacks the ~93% crew turnover at its real (team) level, but it is the highest build effort and drags in FLSA non-exempt / Paycor OT-recalculation complexity the moment cash cascades to crew — too much surface area to launch first.

The hybrid I'd actually ship

  • Spine = Beat Your Best. ~75-80% of the pool is absolute, scored on each patch's 6 KPIs vs its own rolling 3-month baseline, with labor gated on OSAT + kitchen holding.
  • Borrow one ingredient from Mission Control: designate ONE org-weakness as the month's double-weighted spotlight (start with OSAT-gap, then rotate to complaint-SLA → cleanliness). Crucially, make it *double-weight inside the basket*, NOT 70% of the pool — you get the focus, the freshness, and the never-stale rotation without surrendering the balanced-scorecard protection against neglect.
  • Keep the two small social toppers from both: $200/$100/$0 most-improved (multi-prize, no public zero) + a $100 tactic-sharing bounty paid to *both* the sharer and adopter — cheap insurance for cooperation in an N=3 peer group.
  • Default pool $2,400/mo (mid-mandate), hard-capped, ~$600-$900 realistic per-supervisor stake — squarely in the "pay enough" band and fully predictable at ≤$36K/yr, cleanly incremental to the ~$70K quarterly envelope.
  • Sequencing toward All Hands: treat the All Hands crew cascade as the *Phase 2* expansion once Beat Your Best is proven and the FLSA/Paycor OT mechanics are vetted — turnover is the right long-term target, just not the right thing to launch on.

Honesty note for launch: OSAT, complaints, cleanliness, kitchen, GPS visit signals are current enough for monthly scoring; R365 labor lags ~1.5 days, so close the labor gate metric on a fixed cutoff (e.g. through the 2nd of the following month) and stamp every scorecard with its data-through date per house convention.
